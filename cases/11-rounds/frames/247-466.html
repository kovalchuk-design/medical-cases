Research signals
What public research on locum and per diem work showed.
“Lowest bid wins”
Some per diem nursing apps let nurses bid their own hourly rate for a shift, and the lowest bid gets it.
AI Now Institute · Apr 2026
Implication — Rounds never bids. The agent anchors on market data and only negotiates up.
Different pay for the same shift
Researchers warn of “surveillance wages”: platforms using personal data to set individual pay for identical work.
AI Now Institute · 2026
Implication — Benchmarks are shown to the clinician; clinician data never prices them.
No one to call when it goes wrong
In 29 interviews, gig nurses and aides described accounts shut off with little explanation and no supervisor to escalate to.
Roosevelt Institute · Dec 2024
Implication — Every agent action is visible in a thread, and a person can always be reached.
Flexibility is the product
47% find locum work more satisfying than a permanent role; 45% would return to permanent work if schedules and pay improved.
Clinician survey · 2024
Implication — One calendar and conflict detection are first-class.
Regulators are catching up
In 2025 New York classified on-demand nursing platforms as staffing agencies; several stopped operating there.
AI Now Institute · 2026
Implication — Disclosure by design: fees, who pays whom, what the agent did.
Insight
Clinicians don’t lack offers. They lack leverage, visibility and time — and the people helping them are paid by the other side.
