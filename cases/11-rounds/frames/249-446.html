Validation
The approach used to design and refine the solution, with criteria agreed before any session.
Goals
•
Do clinicians trust an agent to counter for them, and at which autonomy level?
•
Is the term sheet understood without a recruiter’s help?
•
Does the tracker reduce credentialing chasing?
Tasks · hero flows
•
Discover: judge an offer and decide in one sitting
•
Booking: approve a counter, sign, upload a missing document
•
Messaging: ask the agent about a term and share a document
Success criteria
•
80%+ unaided task completion
•
Offer decision in under 3 minutes
•
0 wrong signatures or shares
•
SUS 75+ (the average score is 68)
Sample and method
Moderated remote sessions with 8–12 clinicians across three groups (physicians and CRNAs, NPs and PAs, per diem nurses), plus 2–3 expert interviews with a recruiter and a credentialing specialist.
Basis: five participants per distinct user group find most usability problems, and three to four per group when several groups are tested (Nielsen Norman Group). The 68 SUS average comes from a meta-analysis of about 500 studies (Sauro and Lewis).
Iteration plan
Two rounds. Fix every task below 80% after round one, then re-test the changed flows with five new participants. Agent autonomy defaults are set from where participants edit or block a counter.
Assumptions we test
We believe
Clinicians will delegate negotiation to an agent
We’re wrong if — Most clinicians rewrite or block every counter
We believe
Facilities will negotiate with an agent
We’re wrong if — Agent messages are ignored or escalated
We believe
We can benchmark rates credibly
We’re wrong if — Ranges are too wide to act on (more than ±15%)
We believe
Clinicians will pay a 3% fee
We’re wrong if — Under 30% accept after seeing the expected uplift
