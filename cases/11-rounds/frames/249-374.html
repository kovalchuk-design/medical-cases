Opportunity
Three questions framed the design; five principles decide how the agent behaves when the answer isn’t obvious.
How might we
show what an offer is really worth before Maya replies?
negotiate on her behalf without taking control away from her?
make credentialing a one-time effort instead of a per-facility one?
Design principles
Works for the clinician
Paid by the clinician. Never bids a rate down, never sells data.
Explains every recommendation
Each suggestion shows the data behind it and a plain-language why.
Earns autonomy
Starts by asking before every send; clinicians grant more per facility and can take it back.
Leaves a trace
Every action lives in a thread the clinician can read back and share.
Never signs
Signatures, acceptances and document shares need Face ID and a human tap.
The agent does
•
Screens offers against licenses, rate floor and calendar
•
Benchmarks pay and shows its sources
•
Drafts and sends counters within the limits you set
•
Flags risky clauses in plain language
•
Chases credentialing and warns before anything expires
•
Sets aside tax on every payment
The agent doesn’t
•
Bid your rate down — ever
•
Accept, sign or share a document on its own
•
Contact facilities you haven’t approved
•
Give legal or tax advice — it flags, explains and points to a professional
•
Use your data to set your price
